# Corporate sponsorship

RepoSource Registry should approach companies as an open-data infrastructure project, not as an advertising surface.

## Why a company might support it

A developer-tool, AI, research, data, or infrastructure company may benefit from a maintained public repository discovery dataset, documented provenance, and reusable machine-readable artifacts.

Potential sponsor outcomes include:

- Funding maintenance of infrastructure the ecosystem can reuse.
- Supporting data-quality and reproducibility work.
- Enabling better documentation and interoperability.
- Supporting historical snapshots and ecosystem analysis.
- Helping fund integrations that benefit multiple downstream users.

These are potential outcomes, not claims of current customer demand.

## Sponsorship proposal structure

1. Company context: identify the open-source discovery or data problem that matters to the company.
2. Public value: explain which improvement would benefit the wider ecosystem.
3. Project work: define the concrete maintenance or development scope the sponsorship would support.
4. Transparency: keep the dataset methodology and classification rules independent of sponsor identity.
5. Measurement: report objective project outputs such as releases, validation improvements, documentation, coverage of supported artifacts, or infrastructure milestones.

## What sponsorship does not buy

Sponsors do not receive the ability to alter repository rankings, suppress records, insert records, change inclusion rules for private benefit, or obtain private user information.

## Future commercial relationships

If recurring demand emerges, commercial services can be developed separately around higher-volume API access, historical snapshots, analytics, alerts, enterprise exports, or custom integrations. The public dataset should remain useful without purchasing those services.
