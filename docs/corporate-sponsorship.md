# Corporate sponsorship

RepoSource Registry should be presented to companies as public developer-data infrastructure, not as an advertising surface.

## Why a company may support the project

Organizations working in developer tools, AI, data, open source, software engineering, research, or infrastructure may have a legitimate reason to fund a maintained public repository discovery dataset.

The strongest case is straightforward:

**Company objective → public infrastructure need → sponsored engineering work → measurable technical improvement → community benefit**

## Sponsor-funded initiatives

Potential initiatives include:

- stronger data validation and regression coverage;
- reliable CI and public infrastructure;
- better static discovery indexes;
- historical preservation and reproducibility tooling;
- documentation and examples;
- open developer-tool integrations;
- research datasets and ecosystem analysis;
- data-quality tooling that benefits all downstream users.

These are possible sponsored workstreams, not claims that such demand or sponsorship currently exists.

## What sponsors receive

Corporate sponsorship is financial support for public infrastructure. Any recognition should follow the actual sponsorship arrangement and applicable platform rules. Sponsorship does not promise customers, traffic, advertising, leads, search ranking, or exclusivity.

## What sponsorship does not buy

Sponsors cannot:

- alter repository inclusion or exclusion for private benefit;
- change rankings or classifications;
- suppress records;
- create hidden fields or hidden datasets;
- obtain private user information;
- direct the public collection methodology for preferential treatment.

## Proposal structure

A serious sponsorship proposal should define:

1. **Company context** — what developer or data problem motivates support.
2. **Public value** — which project improvement benefits the wider ecosystem.
3. **Technical scope** — the concrete maintenance or development work funded.
4. **Measurement** — objective outputs such as releases, validation coverage, documentation, interoperability, or infrastructure milestones.
5. **Transparency** — confirmation that public data rules remain independent of sponsor identity.

## Commercial services are separate

If a company needs implementation, custom research, integration, or specialized analysis, that work should be contracted separately as professional services. Sponsorship remains support for the public project.
